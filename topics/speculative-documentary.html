Speculative documentary

a strand of moving-image art built on real sites, scientific images, and machine vision, told past the human

Speculative documentary is a distinct strand of contemporary moving-image art, also known as post-human geography or essayistic media art. It sits where investigative research, scientific imaging, and science-fiction world-building meet. Its artists take real scientific data, industrial and military architecture, operational machine vision, and deep-time ecology, and set them inside speculative frames such as future archaeology, post-human narrators, and AI simulation. The aim is to de-centre the human point of view.

This page traces the movement's three foundations, maps its artists in three clusters, and sets out the markers of the work. It began as a field guide brought in from elsewhere. Every artist, title, and year was checked against the sources at the end, and claims that could not be confirmed were cut.

Historical & theoretical lineage

The post-war essay film

Chris Marker and Harun Farocki made film into an open-ended philosophical notebook. Farocki introduced the idea of operational images, pictures made by machines for machines in surveillance, military targeting, and industrial vision, never meant for a human audience.

Pioneers

  • Chris Marker (9 seen, 2 queued) → La Jetée (1962) (seen) · Sans Soleil (1983) (seen)
  • Harun Farocki (11 seen, 1 queued) → Images of the World and the Inscription of War (1988) (seen)

Structural cinema and land art

Michael Snow, Hollis Frampton, and Robert Smithson made duration, scale, geological matter, and the camera itself into the main actors. Landscape and apparatus stopped being a backdrop for human drama.

Pioneers

  • Michael Snow (1 seen)
  • Hollis Frampton (3 seen, 1 queued)
  • Robert Smithson → Spiral Jetty (1970)

Post-human theory and science studies

Donna Haraway, Bruno Latour with Actor-Network Theory, and Gilles Deleuze moved thought away from anthropocentrism. In their frame technology, nuclear waste, minerals, oceans, and animals are active agents that shape planetary futures.

Key thinkers

  • Donna Haraway → A Cyborg Manifesto (1985) · Staying with the Trouble (2016)
  • Bruno Latour
  • Gilles Deleuze

Artist taxonomy

Deep time and geology

Deep-time storage, nuclear decay, mineral evolution, and post-human futures.

  1. Emilija Škarnulytė (12 seen)

    Films the architecture of big science and the Cold War, among them a nuclear power plant, a neutrino observatory, and an abandoned submarine base in Norway, and recasts it as the ruins studied by a future archaeology.

    → Sirenomelia (2018) · t 1/2 (2019) · Burial (2022) (seen)

  2. Deborah Stratman (1 seen, 1 queued)

    Usually directs, shoots, edits, and designs the sound of her own films, tracing how power and control are played out on the land. Last Things (2023) reads evolution and extinction from the point of view of rocks and minerals, in microscopic close-up.

    → In Order Not to Be Here (2002) (list) · The Illinois Parables (2016) · Last Things (2023) (seen)

  3. Katie Paterson

    A Scottish conceptual artist working on a cosmic scale, with records pressed from glacier meltwater, a map of 27,000 dead stars, and Future Library, a hundred-year artwork in Oslo begun in 2014.

Operational images and surveillance

AI systems, machine vision, simulation, and acoustic and spatial investigation.

  1. Hito Steyerl (4 seen) Vitamin V

    Fuses the degraded poor image with game engines and political essayism, moving from documentary to CGI to take on surveillance, visibility, and the circulation of images.

    → How Not to Be Seen: A Fucking Didactic Educational .MOV File (2013) (seen) · Liquidity Inc. (2014) · Factory of the Sun (2015)

  2. Forensic Architecture Vitamin V

    A research group led by Eyal Weizman that turns spatial analysis, 3D modelling, and open-source evidence into investigations of state and corporate violence, published as video investigations, exhibitions, and legal submissions.

  3. Lawrence Abu Hamdan (2 queued) Vitamin V

    Turns listening into evidence. His films, audio installations, and lectures analyse sound in cases of human rights and legal dispute.

    → Rubber Coated Steel (2017) (list) · Walled Unwalled (2018) · The Diary of a Sky (2024) (list)

  4. Jakob Kudsk Steensen

    A Danish artist who builds digital ecosystems in game engines from macro photogrammetry, working with biologists and composers. RE-ANIMATED (2018) resurrects the extinct Kauaʻi ʻōʻō and its island habitat as a virtual-reality installation.

    → RE-ANIMATED (2018) · Berl-Berl (2021)

Ecology and extraction

Marine ecosystems, Indigenous knowledge, climate displacement, and sealed or post-apocalyptic habitats.

  1. Ursula Biemann

    A Swiss artist who carried the video essay from documentary into science fiction, working with Indigenous communities and scientists on borders, water, and planetary ecology. Acoustic Ocean (2018) follows a Sámi biologist-diver listening to the sea off the Lofoten Islands through hydrophones.

    → Forest Law (2014, with Paulo Tavares) · Acoustic Ocean (2018)

  2. John Akomfrah (1 queued) Vitamin V

    Builds essay films and multi-screen installations from archive footage, newly shot material, and sound, on migration, the Black diaspora, memory, and ecology.

    → Vertigo Sea (2015) · Purple (2017)

  3. Ben Rivers (1 queued)

    A British filmmaker who hand-develops much of his 16mm film, so the elements leave their marks on the image. Urth (2016) imagines the last woman on Earth keeping a log inside Biosphere 2 in Arizona.

    → Two Years at Sea (2011) (list) · Urth (2016)

  4. Pierre Huyghe Vitamin V

    Builds living systems that fuse biological and technological elements, treating the exhibition as an environment that changes on its own.

Structural characteristics

  • Machine vision and scientific imaging. Scans, sonar and thermal data, satellite images, and drone feeds sit beside camera footage.
  • A de-centred narrator. The voice belongs to an extinct species, a mineral, a machine, or an archaeologist of the future.
  • Research before a script. Projects start in scientific residencies, archives, and field work.
  • Built for exhibition. Many works are multi-screen installations made for museums and biennials, and they also travel to film festivals.
