Robert
Bresson

Film · France · 1901–1999

In the network · 28 edges

Filmmaker, born in France in 1901. He trained as a painter before cinema. He made thirteen features between 1943 and 1983, among them Diary of a Country Priest (1951), A Man Escaped (1956), Pickpocket (1959), Au hasard Balthazar (1966), Mouchette (1967), and L'Argent (1983).

He worked with non-professional performers he called models and had them repeat their lines until all acting had gone from them. The camera isolates hands, feet, objects, and doors, and much of the story is carried by sound: footsteps, keys, a door closing offscreen. Music is rare. His subjects are grace, imprisonment, and money, often told through a single life moving toward its end. He set down his working rules in Notes on the Cinematograph (1975), a book of aphorisms on ellipsis, sound, and flat line readings.
