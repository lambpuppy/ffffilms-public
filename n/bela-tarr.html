Béla
Tarr

Film · Hungary · 1955–2026

In the network · 13 edges

Filmmaker, born in Pécs in 1955. Family Nest (1979), begun when he was twenty-two, opened a run of social-realist films. From Damnation (1988) he worked with the writer László Krasznahorkai on Damnation, Sátántangó (1994), Werckmeister Harmonies (2000), The Man from London (2007), and The Turin Horse (2011), which he announced as his last film. He later headed the film.factory programme in Sarajevo from 2013 to 2016, and he died in Budapest in January 2026.

The later films are in black and white and built from long takes that walk with their subjects, and Sátántangó runs over seven hours. Rain, mud, wind, and empty villages carry much of the meaning. The camera circles people who wait for something that never comes, while Mihály Víg's repeating music sets the pace. His editor and co-director Ágnes Hranitzky shaped the films with him. The Turin Horse won the Grand Jury Prize at Berlin in 2011.
