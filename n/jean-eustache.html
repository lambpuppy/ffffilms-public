Jean
Eustache

Film · France · 1938–1981

In the network · 4 edges

Filmmaker, born in Pessac in 1938 and raised in the Gironde, partly by his grandmother. He was of the Cinémathèque generation and worked around the Nouvelle Vague before his own films. The short Le Père Noël a les yeux bleus (1966) put Jean-Pierre Léaud on a beach in a Father Christmas costume. The Mother and the Whore (1973) won the Grand Prix at Cannes, and Mes petites amoureuses (1974) followed. He died in 1981, at forty-two.

His films are close to his own life and often take the form of long talk. The Mother and the Whore runs three and a half hours, almost all of it conversation among three people in Paris after 1968. He also moved between documentary and fiction, filming the same village festival twice in La Rosière de Pessac (1968 and 1979). In Numéro zéro (shot in 1971, released in 2003) a fixed camera records his grandmother telling her life, and Une sale histoire (1977) tells one story twice, first as fiction and then as testimony.
