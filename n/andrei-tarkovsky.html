Andrei
Tarkovsky

Film · Soviet Union · 1932–1986

In the network · 13 edges

Filmmaker, born in 1932, the son of the poet Arseny Tarkovsky, whose verses are read in Mirror (1975). He trained at VGIK under Mikhail Romm and made seven features: Ivan's Childhood (1962), Andrei Rublev (1966), Solaris (1972), Mirror, and Stalker (1979), then Nostalghia (1983) in Italy and The Sacrifice (1986) in Sweden after he stayed in the West. He died in Paris in 1986.

His films are built from long takes in which time gathers inside the shot. Water, fire, wind, and ruined rooms recur, along with dreams, childhood memory, and characters caught between faith and doubt. Colour and black and white alternate within a film to separate memory from the present. His book Sculpting in Time argues that the film image is made of duration, with the shot as a pressure of real time instead of a unit of montage. Sven Nykvist, Bergman's cinematographer, photographed The Sacrifice.
