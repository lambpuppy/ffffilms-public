Pier Paolo
Pasolini

Film · Italy · 1922–1975

In the network · 6 edges

Filmmaker, poet, and novelist, born in Bologna in 1922. He published the novel Ragazzi di vita in 1955 and wrote screenplays for others, among them Fellini's Nights of Cabiria, before Accattone (1961) began his run of films. The Gospel According to St. Matthew (1964), Theorem (1968), and Medea (1969) came before the Trilogy of Life: The Decameron (1971), The Canterbury Tales (1972), and Arabian Nights (1974). He was murdered near Ostia in November 1975, and Salò, or the 120 Days of Sodom was released after his death.

He cast non-professional actors from the Roman outskirts and filmed them frontally, with a simplicity he took from early religious painting. His films move between the poor of Rome, myth, and the sacred, and they treat the body as the place where all three meet. Bach plays over the slum scenes of Accattone, and the Gospel sets Christ among southern Italian peasants. His essays on film include 'The Cinema of Poetry' (1965).
