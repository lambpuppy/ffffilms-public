Chris
Marker

Film · France · 1921–2012

In the network · 13 edges

Filmmaker and writer, born Christian François Bouche-Villeneuve in Neuilly-sur-Seine in 1921. He kept his biography deliberately vague. He wrote fiction and film criticism before his first films: Sunday in Peking (1956), Letter from Siberia (1957), and La Jetée (1962), a science-fiction film built almost entirely from still photographs. In 1967 he founded the SLON production group for militant film. He died in Paris in 2012, on his ninety-first birthday.

His films are essays spoken in a voice that is often not quite his own, such as a fictional cameraman's letters in Sans Soleil (1983), read over images from Japan, Guinea-Bissau, Iceland, and San Francisco. Memory, travel, and how images record history are his constant subjects, and A Grin Without a Cat (1977) traces the militant left through its footage. He worked across photography, CD-ROM (Immemory, 1997), and installation, and cats and owls recur throughout the work.
