Yasujirō
Ozu

Film · Japan · 1903–1963

In the network · 12 edges

Filmmaker, born in Tokyo in 1903. He joined Shochiku as an assistant in 1923 and directed his first feature in 1927. The silent comedies led to I Was Born, But... (1932) and The Only Son (1936), his first sound film. After the war came Late Spring (1949), Early Summer (1951), and Tokyo Story (1953), and from Equinox Flower (1958) the colour films, including Floating Weeds (1959) and An Autumn Afternoon (1962), his last. He died in 1963.

His camera sits low and rarely moves, and the cuts fall between scenes instead of inside them. Short shots of empty rooms, trains, and laundry lines separate the scenes. His subject is the family, above all parents and grown children parting, often through a daughter's marriage, told with humour and restraint. He wrote with Kōgo Noda in working sessions away from the studio, and Chishū Ryū appears in nearly all of the films. His grave in Kamakura carries a single character: mu, nothing.
