Michael
Haneke

Film · Austria · 1942–

In the network · 10 edges

Filmmaker, born in Munich in 1942 and raised in Wiener Neustadt outside Vienna. He worked in theatre and television before his first cinema feature, The Seventh Continent (1989). After a move into French-language production he made The Piano Teacher (2001), Caché (2005), The White Ribbon (2009), and Amour (2012). The White Ribbon and Amour each won the Palme d'Or at Cannes.

His films withhold the cues that usually guide an audience's feeling, such as a score, reaction shots, or a sense of relief. The camera stays at a distance that declines to help, and violence often happens offscreen or in one long, unbroken take. His subjects are the bourgeois family, guilt, and the images people consume. Funny Games (1997) turns to address its audience directly, and Caché hides its answer in a crowded final shot.
