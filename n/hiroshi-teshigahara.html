Hiroshi
Teshigahara

Film · Japan · 1927–2001

In the network · 2 edges

Filmmaker, born in Tokyo in 1927, the son of Sōfū Teshigahara, founder of the Sōgetsu school of ikebana. He began in documentary. His fiction features of the 1960s were made with the writer Kōbō Abe, adapting Abe's novels, and the composer Tōru Takemitsu: Pitfall (1962), Woman in the Dunes (1964), The Face of Another (1966), and The Ruined Map (1968). He became head of the Sōgetsu school on his father's death in 1980 and died in 2001.

The Abe films are about identity coming loose. A man is trapped in a sandpit, another is given a new face, and a detective loses himself in the city. He films sand, skin, and surfaces with a documentarist's attention to material, and Takemitsu's scores blur music and noise. After the 1960s he worked largely in ceramics, bamboo installation, and ikebana, while still making films such as Antonio Gaudí (1984), a documentary on the Catalan architect, and Rikyu (1989).
