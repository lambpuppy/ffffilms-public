Apichatpong
Weerasethakul

Film · Thailand · 1970–

In the network · 9 edges

Filmmaker, born in Bangkok in 1970. He trained in architecture at Khon Kaen and then in film in Chicago, with an MFA from the School of the Art Institute in 1997. His features run from Blissfully Yours (2002) and Tropical Malady (2004) to Uncle Boonmee Who Can Recall His Past Lives (2010), the first Thai film to win the Palme d'Or, Cemetery of Splendour (2015), and Memoria (2021), made in Colombia with Tilda Swinton.

Most of the films are set in the Isan northeast, among hospitals, jungles, and spirits, and the dead appear in daylight without ceremony. Many are split into two halves, where a daytime story turns into myth or dream. Light and sound are treated as physical presences, and the takes are calm and unhurried. He also makes gallery installations. Syndromes and a Century (2006) ran into Thai censorship demands that he refused, and much of his work since has been made with international money outside the Thai system.
