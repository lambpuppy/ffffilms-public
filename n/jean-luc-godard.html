Jean-Luc
Godard

Film · France / Switzerland · 1930–2022

In the network · 21 edges

Filmmaker, born in Paris in 1930. He wrote for Cahiers du cinéma before Breathless (1960), then made roughly a feature a year through 1967, including Vivre sa vie (1962), Contempt (1963), Bande à part (1964), Pierrot le fou (1965), and Week End (1967). After 1968 he left commercial cinema for the Dziga Vertov group with Jean-Pierre Gorin and then video in the 1970s. Histoire(s) du cinéma (1988–1998) and late 3D work such as Goodbye to Language (2014) and The Image Book (2018) followed. He died in 2022.

His method throughout was collage: jump cuts, on-screen titles, quotations from books and paintings, and sound that breaks away from the image. He addressed the audience directly, used primary colours as signs, and let politics, philosophy, and love stories share the same scene. From the 1970s his films became essays that think through images, and Histoire(s) du cinéma rewrites the history of cinema out of its own fragments.
