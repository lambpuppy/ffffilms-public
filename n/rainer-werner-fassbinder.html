Rainer Werner
Fassbinder

Film · Germany · 1945–1982

In the network · 13 edges

Filmmaker, born in Bavaria in 1945. He worked in theatre first, with the Munich antiteater, and then made films from 1969: roughly forty features for cinema and television before his death in 1982, at thirty-seven. Ali: Fear Eats the Soul (1974), The Marriage of Maria Braun (1979), the fifteen-hour Berlin Alexanderplatz (1980), and the BRD trilogy closed the run.

He wrote on Douglas Sirk's melodramas and remade All That Heaven Allows as Ali: Fear Eats the Soul, turning the genre's machinery on German history and on his own household. His frames are crowded with doorways, mirrors, and windows that box the characters in, and the performances are stylised. His subjects are love as exploitation, outsiders, and the West German economic miracle. The speed was part of the method, with schedules measured in weeks, one troupe of actors, and the same rooms.
