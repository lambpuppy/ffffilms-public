Ingmar
Bergman

Film · Sweden · 1918–2007

In the network · 8 edges

Filmmaker, born in Uppsala in 1918, the son of a Lutheran pastor. The parsonage household recurs through the work. He worked in theatre from the 1940s, in Helsingborg, Gothenburg, Malmö, and for decades at Stockholm's Dramaten, alongside some forty features for cinema and television. These include The Seventh Seal and Wild Strawberries (both 1957), the Faith trilogy (1961–63), Persona (1966), Cries and Whispers (1972), and Fanny and Alexander (1982). He died on Fårö in 2007.

His films are chamber dramas about faith, doubt, illness, and marriage, often with a small cast in an enclosed space. He films faces in long close-ups and treats them as landscapes, and dreams and memories cut into the present. He worked with a repertory company that included Liv Ullmann, Bibi Andersson, and Max von Sydow. Sven Nykvist photographed most of the films from the 1960s on, taking over from Gunnar Fischer. The island of Fårö, first used in Through a Glass Darkly, became his home and his subject.
