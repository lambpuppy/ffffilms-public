Breathless

Film · France · 1960 · 90 min

Michel, a small-time thief who has stolen a car and shot a policeman, drifts through Paris quoting Bogart. He tries to get Patricia, a student selling the Herald Tribune on the Champs-Élysées, to say she loves him. Jean-Paul Belmondo and Jean Seberg star, and Raoul Coutard shot it, often from a wheelchair.

It was made in about four weeks from a story outline by Truffaut, and much of it was improvised. Its jump cuts, into and out of scenes mid-movement, dropped the connecting material that commercial cinema smoothed over.
