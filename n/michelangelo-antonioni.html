Michelangelo
Antonioni

Film · Italy · 1912–2007

In the network · 8 edges

Filmmaker, born in Ferrara in 1912. He began as a film critic, at Il Corriere Padano and then at the journal Cinema in Rome, and moved through documentary to features from 1950. The films that established him came in quick succession with Monica Vitti: L'Avventura (1960), La Notte (1961), L'Eclisse (1962), and Il deserto rosso (1964). Blow-Up (1966), made in English in London, and The Passenger (1975) followed, with long gaps afterwards. He died in 2007.

His characters drift through marriages, jobs, and cities without finding what they want. His shots wait after the action is over, letting architecture, empty streets, and weather carry what the dialogue holds back. L'Avventura loses its central character and never finds her, and L'Eclisse ends with seven minutes of the street corner where its lovers failed to meet. In Il deserto rosso he painted real locations to use colour as a state of mind.
