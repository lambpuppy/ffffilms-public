Claire
Denis

Film · France · 1948–

In the network · 8 edges

Filmmaker, born in Paris in 1948. She grew up in colonial French Africa, where her father was a civil servant, and worked as assistant director to Wim Wenders on Paris, Texas and Jim Jarmusch on Down by Law before her debut, Chocolat (1988). Beau Travail (1999), on a Foreign Legion unit in Djibouti, became her most-cited film, and 35 Shots of Rum (2008), White Material (2009), High Life (2018), and Stars at Noon (2022) followed.

Her films put bodies before plot. They are filmed close, so touch and movement carry the story, and much of the explanation is left out. Colonial histories, outsiders, families, and desire recur, and she moves freely between genres, from horror to science fiction. She works with a regular team, including the cinematographer Agnès Godard and the band Tindersticks on the music. The films often turn on rhythm and music, as in the dance that ends Beau Travail.
