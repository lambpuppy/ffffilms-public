Luis
Buñuel

Film · Spain / Mexico · 1900–1983

In the network · 14 edges

Filmmaker, born in Calanda, Aragón, in 1900. At Madrid's Residencia de Estudiantes he knew Salvador Dalí and Federico García Lorca, and Un Chien Andalou (1929) and L'Âge d'or (1930), made with Dalí, brought him into the surrealist circle. After the Spanish Civil War he settled in Mexico, where Los Olvidados (1950) began a run of low-budget films. From Viridiana (1961), shot in Spain and banned there, he worked between Spain, France, and Italy on films such as Belle de Jour (1967), The Discreet Charm of the Bourgeoisie (1972), and That Obscure Object of Desire (1977). He died in Mexico City in 1983.

His films keep surrealism's dream logic but film it in a plain, calm style, so the strange things happen in ordinary light. The Church, the bourgeoisie, and desire are his constant targets. Dinner guests cannot leave a room, a beggar may be a saint, and two actresses play one woman. The Discreet Charm of the Bourgeoisie won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film.
