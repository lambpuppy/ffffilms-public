Roberto
Gavaldón

Film · Mexico · 1909–1986

In the network · 1 edge

Filmmaker, born in Jiménez, Chihuahua, in 1909. He came up through Mexican studio film from the 1930s and directed some forty features. The Kneeling Goddess (1947), Rosauro Castro (1950), In the Palm of Your Hand (1951), and Macario (1960) are the run the collection holds. He also made The Littlest Outlaw (1955) for Walt Disney, shot in Mexico. He died in 1986.

His films are melodramas and noirs of Mexico's golden age, often about obsession, guilt, and fate. He worked with the cinematographer Gabriel Figueroa, whose deep shadows and dramatic skies give the films much of their look. Macario, about a poor woodcutter who strikes a bargain with Death on the Day of the Dead, turns folk tale into a gothic fable. It was the first Mexican film nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film.
